The buttons on your iPhone aren't
blurring what's behind them. They're
bending it, like a lens. Apple calls
this liquid glass, a digital material
that bends and shapes light in real
time. Imagine you're scrolling through
your photos and a bright sunset slides
under the tab bar. The old menus, all
the way back to iOS 7, were frosted
glass. They smeared the colors behind
them into a soft blur, which Apple says
scatter the light. Liquid glass doesn't
smear anything. It warps the sunset, the
way a glass of water warps the world
behind it, and Apple calls that lensing.
That warping is how your eye finds the
button. It shows you its shape and where
it ends with no solid box around it. But
a lens [snorts] alone would be hard to
read, so Apple stacked layers on top.
Virtual lights shine on the glass, and
in some places they move when you tilt
your phone. A shadow gets darker when
text goes under it and lighter over a
plain white page. The tint keeps
shifting so the buttons stay readable.
And when your sunset turns into a night
shot, the whole bar flips from light to
dark. Open a menu and the glass acts
thicker, bending the light more with
deeper shadows. Touch it and it lights
up from inside, starting right under
your finger. And a button never just
[snorts] fades in. It appears by slowly
turning up the bending so it seems to
form out of the screen. If it's too much
for you, open settings, appearance,
liquid glass, and slide it toward tinted
for more solid glass and more contrast.
So your buttons were never a blurry
window over your photos. They're a lens
and your photos are the lighted bends.
